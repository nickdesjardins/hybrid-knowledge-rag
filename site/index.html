Applied AI · hybrid RAG · verified citations

Questions about SOPs. Answers you can audit.

This service answers questions over dense procedures and regulatory manuals. It finds the governing passages with vector and keyword search, re-ranks them with a cross-encoder, and lets a language model answer only by quoting the text. Every quote is then checked against the stored document, and the page number comes from the database, not from the model. When the documents do not say it, the answer says so.

Built to be read. Each recorded answer below opens into a step-by-step walkthrough with its own numbers, including a map of where the question landed among the stored chunks and how the four rankings disagreed about them. The rank-fusion lab lets you try the core formula, and every concept links to the code that implements it.

How a question is answered

Step 1 happens once per document, when it is ingested. Steps 2 to 5 happen for every question; the walkthrough under each recorded run above shows them with real numbers.

  1. 1

    Ingest with context

    PDFs are split along headings and paragraphs with a sliding overlap. Each chunk gets a short situational preamble (document, section, pages) before it is embedded and indexed, so a line like “the limit is 10 ppm” stays findable on its own.

    ingestion/pipeline.py

  2. 2

    Search two ways at once

    One SQL statement runs a pgvector cosine search over an HNSW index and a PostgreSQL full-text search over a GIN index, then fuses both lists with Reciprocal Rank Fusion.

    retrieval/hybrid_search.py

  3. 3

    Re-rank

    A cross-encoder reads the question and each candidate together and keeps the three best. This is what lifts the right clause above look-alikes.

    retrieval/reranker.py

  4. 4

    Answer under a schema

    The model must return JSON: an answer, a confidence score, and quotes copied character for character, or an explicit “insufficient context”.

    generator/grounded_llm.py

  5. 5

    Verify

    The server looks for every quote in the stored chunk and drops what does not match. Titles and page numbers are filled from the database. An answer that claims grounding but has no surviving quote is withheld.

    generator/grounded_llm.py

Why plain vector search is not enough

    

    

      

        
Context collapse

        
A chunk cut out of a manual loses its document, its section and its subject. Embedding it that way makes two near-identical clauses from different SOPs indistinguishable. Prepending context before indexing fixes both retrievers at once.

      

      

        
Keyword blind spots

        
Embeddings capture meaning and blur identifiers. Compliance questions are full of them: 21 CFR Part 11, ISO 2859-1, SOP-QA-017. Full-text search catches the exact match; rank fusion merges the two lists without calibrating their scores.

      

      

        
Lost in the middle

        
Handing a model fifty passages degrades its attention to the ones in the middle. Re-ranking down to three passages raises faithfulness and cuts the prompt to a fraction.

      

      

        
Ungrounded answers

        
Even with the right passage, a model can misquote a number or cite a page it never saw. Here a quote that is not in the stored text is discarded, and the model never supplies a page number at all.
